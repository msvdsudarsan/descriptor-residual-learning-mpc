# Physics-informed residual learning of descriptor power-network models with enforced algebraic constraints: prediction, contingency margins and a control test

Code, data and results for the manuscript prepared for *Electric Power Systems Research* (Elsevier), release 2.1.0, November 2026. This release adds the recalibrated-physics comparison in the voltage-magnitude variant (`extensions/ac_recal/`) and the exploratory contingency diagnostics (`extensions/contingency_mechanism/`). This release is archived on Zenodo, DOI: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24167124. The previous release 2.0.0 is archived on Zenodo, DOI: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24142079; it remains available and does not contain these two folders.

## Reproduce
```bash
git clone https://github.com/msvdsudarsan/descriptor-residual-learning-mpc.git && cd descriptor-residual-learning-mpc
pip install -r requirements.txt
bash run_all.sh        # run time depends on the hardware; the scalar closed-loop stage and case39 are the longest
```
All random numbers use fixed seeds and nothing is downloaded. Timing columns (`solve_ms`) are hardware-dependent. On other machines tiny floating-point differences can also occur in the other columns (up to about 0.3% in the extreme case39 atlas corners, load scale 3), without changing any value reported in the manuscript. `python tools/check_paper_values.py` compares the regenerated results with the numbers in the paper.

## Model summary
* Scalar benchmark: one angle/frequency pair and one algebraic voltage-like variable; the physics model has wrong gains and omits the cubic term.
* Networks: classical swing generators, all bus angles algebraic (lossless network, unit voltages). Inertia, damping and transient reactance are **declared assumptions** (they are not in MATPOWER); see Supplementary Material S1 (parameter table).
* Variants: A0 physics-only (tangent update), A1 + learned residual, A2 + Newton projection, A3 + penalty tightening by the prediction-error envelope; C0 static feedback.

## Limits (see the paper)
Reduced models, no electromagnetic-transient validation; the residual transfers across loadings when loading and dispatch are known but not under an unannounced dispatch shift; the residual models are thresholded, not sparse, on the networks; the envelope is an empirical margin and not a guarantee; a network-constraint gain from the penalty tightening was not demonstrated in the tested scenarios (on case9 the tightened limit stays above the trajectory peak, on case39 the reason why A3 and A2 give identical inputs was not established); with finite-difference gradients the case39 controller is slower than the sampling period; the analytic gradient (A2, A3) brings it below on the machine used, and A0 and A1 still use finite differences; the trust gate and the reconciliation are heuristics without a guarantee.

## Scientific scope

This repository reproduces the numerical evidence for structure-preserving residual learning, descriptor-manifold projection, the validity map, trust gating, dispatch reconciliation and exact-gradient predictive control reported in the manuscript. It makes no claim beyond the reduced models and the tested scenarios.

## Added for the second manuscript version (baselines and load dynamics)
`code/network_baselines_loaddyn.py` compares the sparse weak-form residual with a sparse strong-form fit, a dense ridge fit and a small neural network in the same Newton-projected predictor, and tests an unmodelled first-order load lag, a 1.5 times larger saliency term and training noise of 1e-3, 1e-2 and 3e-2 (prediction only, reduced models). `code/make_baseline_figure.py` draws `figures/Figure_baselines.*` and writes `results/table_baselines.tex`. `code/application_example_case39.py` combines stored results into the numbers of the application example (manuscript Section 7). `tests/test_baselines.py` and `tests/test_application.py` check the manuscript statements against the stored CSV files. The four residual models are close in accuracy; the weak form is not more accurate than the strong form in these tests.


## Added for the application section (39-bus New England network)
`code/worked_example_case39.py` prints the six-step solved example of the manuscript (equilibrium, regularity bound, one weak-form equation, Newton projection, one-second prediction, gate and reconciliation) and writes `results/worked_example_case39.json`. `tools/detectability_check.py` checks the first-order detectability remark (lowest-order slope against the finite-difference slope, smallest visible dispatch step, one Gauss-Newton step on the dispatch scale). `code/application_case39.py scan` adds unannounced steps of 2, 4, 6, 8 and 15 percent to the persistent steps of `network_reconcile.py`; `code/application_case39.py authority <scale>` runs the closed loop after a 5 percent step with the input bound multiplied by `scale`; `code/merge_authority.py` joins the three scales and `code/make_application_figure.py` draws `figures/Figure_application.*` and writes `results/case39_application_scan_merged.csv`. `tests/test_application.py` checks the quoted numbers. The closed-loop solution times of the authority runs are not quoted because the three scales were run in parallel on two cores.

`tools/gradient_clip_check.py` repeats the gradient check with the residual clipping lowered until it is active for most samples and writes `results/gradient_clip_check.csv`. `tests/run_tests.py` runs the test files one by one and prints the time of each. The gradient is exact for the discrete projected predictor as coded, up to the Newton tolerance, and it is one-sided on the clip boundary.


## Changes after the independent audit (October 2026)
1. Trust gate: the trust weight now scales the clipped residual, d_eff = tau * clip(d), as stated in the manuscript. Before, the coefficients were scaled and the original clip limit was restored (`network_reconcile.py`, `network_validity_gate.py`). Rerunning every gate, reconciliation, atlas and recovery script gave no change in any reported number.
2. Error margin: the network margin is the largest componentwise error over all line angles and all rotor-to-bus angles. Before, it was the difference between the largest predicted and the largest true line angle (`network_experiments.py`). The margins grow (case9 elevated loading, step 5: 0.0150 to 0.023 rad; case39: 0.0157 to 0.0463 rad at step 5). Three manuscript values change: envelope 3.1 to 3.6 degrees, A3 time above the limit 3.6 to 3.8 percent, A3 violation 0.70 to 0.75. The closed-loop controller comparison does not change (a constraint gain from tightening was not demonstrated).
3. `tests/test_audit_fixes.py` tests both points. `tools/update_envelope_tables.py` rewrites the supplement envelope tables from the CSV files.
4. `tests/test_props_independent.py` checks Propositions 2 and 3 with separately written formulas (random states inside the 30 degree region); no violation, and the Lipschitz constant is conservative by a factor of about 13 to 16. `tools/closed_loop_intervals.py` writes paired bootstrap intervals of the closed-loop speed change to `results/closed_loop_intervals.csv`.
5. Calibration with independent trajectories. `code/network_conformal_independent.py` draws disjoint calibration sets of fresh static-law trajectories (case9: 400 sets of 20, case39: 160) and one fresh test pool (2000 and 1000) with seeds 40000 (pilot), 50000+i (calibration), 60000 (test), 70000 (shifted pool); coverage of the trajectory-level split-conformal envelope equals the guaranteed level for n of at least 20 and is about 10/11 for n=10; under a doubled load fluctuation it is 0.15 to 0.20. The earlier version of this check resampled one finite pool and was removed.
6. Policy-matched recalibration. `code/network_cl_recalibration.py` recalibrates the closed-loop envelope with controller A2, the generator of the closed-loop study and disjoint seeds (pilot 80000, calibration 81000+i, test 82000; the evaluation realizations use 6001). Result: the 7-trajectory envelope was conservative, the new envelope has the guaranteed marginal coverage, and controller A3 with it is identical to A2.
7. Parametric baseline. `code/network_param_baseline.py` compares the library residual with damping (plus constant) and damping plus cubic speed (plus constant) corrections. The two- and three-coefficient corrections match the library residual in these models. A damping-only fit without a constant is biased and is not used.
`code/make_calibration_tables.py` writes the three tables and `figures/Figure_conformal.*`; `tools/check_calibration_values.py` checks the numbers quoted in the manuscript against the CSV files (`paper/values_calibration.json`); `tests/test_calibration.py` checks the stored results.
