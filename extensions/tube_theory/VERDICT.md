# VERDICT (updated after the second, "make it tight" attempt)
Bottom line: a deterministic closed-loop/tube guarantee that is TIGHT and unconditional is not achievable by these methods; what I could prove is (i) a much tighter bound that needs calibrated (data-derived) constants and a physics-type predictor (4.8x of the observed maximum at 0.5 s), (ii) a finite-sample conformal-hybrid coverage theorem that is valid but NOT tighter than the paper's direct envelope, (iii) a clean diagnosis of where the looseness lives. The closed-loop test shows a real tightening effect only at 4x actuator authority.

## Step 1  diagnosis (THEORY.md Sec. 8)
Per-window ratio at 0.5 s, case9: Thm 1 = 497x median window (108x vs max-observed). Costs: worst-case load vs actual 4.8x; sup rho vs actual 1.3x; uniform load-force constant 1.7x; Omega_bar 1.2x; residual-Lipschitz perturbation 1.9x; and 14.7x that remains even when every ingredient is the actual one = energy->span conversion, because 99 % of the energy is centre-of-inertia kinetic energy that moves no algebraic angle. Removing COI + actual projected forcing gives 2.4x. Attained worst case over the load class (lamp 0.08, omega 1..6): 0.0188 rad vs Thm 1's 1.174 rad (62x) vs paper envelope 0.0116 rad.

"Calibrated" = constants (residual Lipschitz box, load-force and load-angle constants phi, Lam) taken as sup over recorded states and treated as hypotheses; checked on held-out windows (0 violations) but NOT proved. With only rigorous constants nothing is below 43x. I cannot get a tight unconditional bound because (1) a class-wide statement must cover the worst load in the class, (2) uniform state-independent constants (Cauchy-Schwarz/Loewner) cost 1.7-4x, (3) a stiffness perturbation of the learned residual cannot be excluded from resonating in a lightly damped oscillator.

## Step 3  closed loop (T-B), case9, load scale 2.75, limit 23 deg, 10 paired realizations, mean +- 95 % interval [closed_loop_tb_x*.log, results/closed_loop_tb_x*.csv]
Controllers: A2 (no margin), A3p (paper conformal envelope, 1.3-4.4 deg), A3h (hybrid H2, 1.7-8.2 deg). Paired difference to A2:

| x2 | 0.056 | +0.054 +- 0.034 (worse) | +0.079 +- 0.034 (worse) | +0.23 / +0.33 deg | rotor-error rms 0.024 -> 0.041 / 0.069 |

Plainly: no effect at the nominal authority; harmful at x2 (the margin makes the controller fight the angle limit with an input that is still nearly saturated, u_rms/umax 0.77-0.84, and increases oscillation: this is the measured pattern, the causal story is a hypothesis, not tested); a real reduction of violation time (5.1 % -> 0.5 %) and peak (23.4 -> 22.3-22.4 deg) only at x4, bought with 3.4x (A3p) to 5.2x (A3h) larger rotor-angle deviation. The hybrid margin gives the same violation reduction as the paper's envelope at x4 and costs more, and is worse at x2: it brings nothing in closed loop. Physical reading: at x1 the controller is saturated almost all the time (u_rms/umax = 0.92, omega rms 0.9) just damping the swing, so there is no headroom left to respond to a tightened limit inside the 0.5 s horizon; headroom appears only at x4 (u_rms/umax 0.44). The raw acceleration umax/M (~4.7 rad/s^2) is not small, so "authority within the horizon" is about headroom after the damping duty, not about the maximum torque; I did not run an experiment isolating this.

## Step 4  what is now proved, tight, vacuous
- PROVED and reasonably tight (4.8x of observed at 0.5 s, 0 violations): Thm 3/4 for the idealised physics predictor with calibrated constants (case9). Not tight (14-38x): learned predictor on case9/case39/stress. Not tight (43-125x): any unconditional version.
- PROVED, valid but not tighter than existing practice: Thm 2.
- Vacuous/failed: Thm 1 as the headline (kept in THEORY.md Part I as a baseline), cross-term for the learned residual, a transfer-function load bound.

## Novelty impact (self-rating 4/10)
- Honest estimate: +0.5 to +1 at most (4 -> 4.5-5), conditional on presenting it correctly. The new pieces with some content: the COI-removed Metzler bound with explicit constants (no damping-sign condition), the quantified diagnosis (99 % of the energy is COI kinetic energy; class-attained worst case 0.0188 rad), the finite-sample hybrid theorem (correct, but it does not improve the margin), the T-C scope conditions. The closed-loop evidence is mixed (benefit only at 4x authority, harm at 2x), which does not support a "closed-loop constraint satisfaction" claim.
- What I would NOT claim in the paper: a tight guarantee, or any benefit at the nominal authority. What is publishable as an honest remark: "the conformal margin is not improved by a structure-based transfer; the conformal envelope, calibrated on closed-loop-like data, is the tighter object; structure explains why one scalar suffices and gives scope conditions (cos>0, no generator-less islands, no unmodelled outages)".
